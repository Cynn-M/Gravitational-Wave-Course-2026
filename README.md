# Gravitational Wave Course — 2026

Computational projects, numerical experiments, and analytical calculations developed as part of the **2026 Gravitational Waves Course** at the **Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (Albert Einstein Institute)**.

**Course:** [2026 Gravitational Waves](https://imprs-lectures.aei.mpg.de/2026-gravitational-waves/)

This repository collects computational implementations and homework projects exploring different aspects of gravitational physics, including black hole dynamics, gravitational-wave emission, numerical methods, and the Effective-One-Body (EOB) formalism.

## Repository Structure

### Python

Numerical implementations and computational projects related to black hole physics and gravitational-wave analysis.

- **[Black Hole Kicks](Python/Black_Hole_Kicks/)**  
  Analysis of gravitational-wave multipoles and the recoil velocity of remnant black holes using numerical-relativity simulations from the SXS catalog.

- **[Numerical ADM Evolution of Black Holes](Python/Numerical_ADM_Evolution_of_Black_Holes/)**  
  Implementation of numerical methods, including finite differences, Runge–Kutta integration, and the method of lines, applied to the ADM evolution of a spherically symmetric Schwarzschild black hole.

### Mathematica

Symbolic calculations and analytical derivations related to relativistic two-body dynamics and gravitational-wave emission.

- **[EOB Hamiltonian and Radiation Reaction](Mathematica/)**  
  Construction of the Effective-One-Body Hamiltonian, canonical transformations, post-Newtonian matching, gravitational-wave energy and angular momentum fluxes, and orbital averaging.

## Topics

- Gravitational-wave emission and multipolar decomposition.
- Black hole dynamics and recoil (kick) velocities.
- Numerical relativity and the SXS catalog.
- ADM formulation and numerical evolution of black hole spacetimes.
- Effective-One-Body (EOB) formalism.
- Post-Newtonian dynamics and radiation reaction.
- Energy and angular momentum balance laws.

## Languages and Tools

- **Python:** NumPy, Matplotlib, Pandas, SXS.
- **Wolfram Mathematica:** symbolic calculations and analytical derivations.
- **Numerical methods:** finite differences, RK4, and method of lines.
- **Version control:** Git and GitHub.
